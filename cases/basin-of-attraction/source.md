BASIN OF ATTRACTION (CLOSED CAPTIONED FOR THOSE WHO DON’T)  

Forty years into this drifting opera and the cast list still writes itself in the margins of my skull. There’s always that secret chord under the machinery—but you don’t really care for spirit, do you? You care for the downbeat that tells you when to clap.  

The house lights never came back after September 10, 2025. A campus mic turned elegy; a body hit the floor; the nation filmed itself filming. The narrative set like plaster: statements, candles, scapegoats, reels spun out like cheap taffy. “Don’t make a set piece of mourning,” we say, while the proscenium gets rebuilt on schedule. Outrage is the only lighting rig still in the budget.  

Elsewhere, the man in the stole says he hopes his wife will be moved by the Gospel. Swoons here, sirens there, and the upload clocked like doctrine. The seam between wish and program is thinner than a mic cable; the pretense of a wall keeps the hands clean.  

Yale—Cambridge—Yale. That’s ink, not cosplay. We anoint with diplomas now; accuracy is a sacrament.  

Inside my pantheon the plastic never cools. Mecha Shiva swings four chrome arms—piety, grievance, venture logic, hillbilly drag—while Matrix Jesus mutters no kings, not then, not now, not after the warning about sons and vineyards. Marxist Jesus kicks the lectern: acts, not vibes; tables and teleprompters both.  

You already know the choreography: work and shoot, heel and face, a pact that says the feelings stay real even when the script is written longhand. When the ghosts tap the mat, we rename the fixture. Rename the cage, keep the cage.  

Uriah Heep sidles on with ’umble hands, all starch and deference; Uriah Heep kicks the monitors and the room starts to hum—very ’eavy, very ’umble—until humility cashes out in deeds and percentages. The bow is just a chokehold taught etiquette.  

People ask why it’s hard to believe life lifts from the draft. I point at a roof, a bath, a letter. A soldier moved to the front, then moved out of the way, the messenger rehearsed on the stairs. We canonized it and pretended the melody absolved the plot. Crown energy eats and eats and never clears its plate.  

If you want space Jesus, fine. The manual says one wife now and forever, and the footnotes argue in the wings. Planets on layaway or not, the point is custody of the story.  

Copperfield takes the stage. Depending on your seat, he vanishes a statue or a debt. The patter is policy. The patina is piety. The audience stares where the hand points; the turntable rotates. Liberty is not where you left it.  

So here’s the tableau: a widow at the podium, a baritone tuned for minor keys, Heep bowing and touring at once, Matrix Jesus rewriting the dimmer pack, Marxist Jesus stapling a call to acts to the door, the crew swapping canvases between segments, and you mouthing along because resolution feels like faith when it’s only choreography. But you don’t really care for spirit, do you? You care for the crown, rebranded as closure.  

The counter‑ritual stays brutal and simple: no kings. No plots that clear the pipe with blood. No prayer that sounds like a press release. Hear the chord—don’t let it turn into an alibi.
