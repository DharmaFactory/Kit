## Learnings

**What worked:**
- [#pattern] Centering the design on agent perspective ("what does Codex experience?" vs. "what does the protocol need?") clarified the UX immediately
- [#pattern] Human's clarification ("all input is just input") unlocked the simple insight: messages are indistinguishable from terminal input, filesystem is just transport

**Patterns noticed:**
- [#pattern] Terminal-native designs (stdin/stdout/files) feel more peer-to-peer than API-based designs, even when both are technically peer-to-peer
- [#pattern] MCP tool-call model is simpler than filesystem polling: active delivery vs. passive checking, single tool vs. multiple commands
- [#pattern] Protocol-first thinking (like Erlang/OTP) enables composability: agents as MCP servers, nested registries, federation
- [#pattern] Capability-based discovery ("who can do X?") is more flexible than fixed topology or hardcoded addresses
- [#pattern] Separating "what" (protocol spec) from "how" (implementation) clarifies scope and prevents hand-waving complexity
